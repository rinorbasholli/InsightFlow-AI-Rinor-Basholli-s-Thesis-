\prefacesection{Abstract}
Generative artificial intelligence lowers the cost of phishing, deepfakes and adversarial attacks, yet students usually meet these risks in separate tools that rarely distinguish measured results from model-generated text. This report presents InsightFlow AI, a web laboratory that places AI-generated phishing, deepfake scenarios and adversarial attacks in one authenticated workflow and labels every analytical output with its provenance (\texttt{gemini}, \texttt{fallback}, \texttt{preset} or \texttt{computed}). The laboratory consists of a React client, an Express server that calls the Gemini API and enforces authentication, rate limits, request-size limits and prompt-injection mitigations, and a Python service that runs FGSM and PGD attacks on a pretrained CIFAR-10 ResNet-56. Two reproducible evaluations were carried out. On a constructed corpus of 187 institutional emails in which legitimate and phishing messages share their wording, a TF-IDF and logistic-regression classifier using the wording alone reached a cross-validated F1 of 0.708, below an always-phishing baseline of 0.736. Adding the sender address raised F1 to 0.806, but when the sender domains were unseen in training, F1 fell to 0.743, level with the baseline, while AUC remained 0.784. On 200 CIFAR-10 test images, a clean accuracy of 0.955 fell to 0.05 under PGD at $\varepsilon = 2/255$ and to 0 at $8/255$, with every perturbation verified to lie within its budget. All 34 automated tests pass. The results indicate that, on this corpus, wording does not separate phishing from legitimate mail and the sender's advantage depends largely on known domains, and that undefended classifiers are fragile; the educational effect of the laboratory and the accuracy of its LLM-based email analysis remain to be evaluated.

\textbf{Keywords:} cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, phishing, deepfakes, adversarial examples, security education.
