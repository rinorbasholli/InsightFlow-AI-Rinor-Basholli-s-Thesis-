\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:rq-answers}

\paragraph{RQ1.} On the reconstructed corpus, the wording of a message contributes little to separating phishing from legitimate mail: a wording-only classifier reached a cross-validated F1 of $0.708 \pm 0.020$, below the always-phishing baseline of $0.736 \pm 0.008$, with an AUC of $0.629$. Adding the sender address raised F1 to $0.806 \pm 0.040$ and AUC to $0.837$. When legitimate and phishing messages share their wording, the sender carries the signal. This finding is limited to the corpus, whose phishing messages were written by the author.

\paragraph{RQ2.} A deepfake module can support awareness teaching without processing media by presenting scenario-based cues and defences with an explicit provenance label (preset, LLM narration or fallback). The limit of this design is that it teaches procedures, not detection, and its effect on learners was not measured.

\paragraph{RQ3.} An undefended, pretrained CIFAR-10 ResNet-56 is highly vulnerable: clean accuracy of 0.955 falls to 0.45 under FGSM and 0.05 under PGD at $\varepsilon = 2/255$, and PGD reduces accuracy to 0 at $8/255$ and $16/255$, with every perturbation within its budget.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

In order of priority, the following work would most strengthen the laboratory and its evaluation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Evaluate the LLM-based email analysis on the benchmark corpus and report its accuracy next to the TF-IDF models.
\item Add a test set of phishing emails generated by an LLM through the laboratory's own generator, and measure whether models trained on author-written emails detect them.
\item Obtain independent labels for a sample of the corpus from a second annotator and report the agreement.
\item Carry out a controlled study with learners, following the design of earlier anti-phishing training studies~\cite{sheng2007phil}.
\item Add output-side controls to the generator and a data-protection notice to the analysis module.
\item Evaluate the preprocessing defences that the laboratory recommends against adaptive attacks~\cite{guo2018countering,athalye2018obfuscated}.
\item Pin the model weights and library versions, move sessions and rate limits to a shared store for multi-instance deployment, and add end-to-end HTTP tests.
\end{enumerate}
